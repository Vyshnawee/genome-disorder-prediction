About this project

What it does

GeneScope predicts which of five genetic diseases a patient most likely has, from routine lab results and genetic markers:

  • Thalassemia: low hemoglobin, high fetal hemoglobin
  • Hemophilia: inherited clotting-factor disorder
  • Breast Cancer (BRCA1/p53): high BRCA1 expression, p53 mutation
  • Sickle Cell Anemia: high share of sickled red cells
  • Cystic Fibrosis: high sweat chloride

How it works

  1. Data: Genetic Disease Prediction Dataset from Kaggle (1,000 patients, 12 features, CC-BY-SA-4.0). The original file is kept untouched and a working copy is used.
  2. Clean and validate: checks for missing values, duplicates and invalid labels.
  3. Split: 20% of patients are held back and never used in training.
  4. Train: five models are compared with 5-fold cross-validation, and the one with the most reliable probabilities is kept.
  5. Evaluate: final scores come from the held-back patients only.

Reading the result

Confidence is the model's probability for its top answer.

  • High 80% or more
  • Moderate 50–80%
  • Low below 50%. Treat the result as uncertain.

Missing tests are filled with typical values, so results are more reliable when the key marker for each disease is entered.

Limitations

The dataset is synthetic but realistic (as stated by its author) and contains 1,000 patients. Every patient has one of the five diseases, so the model always picks one of them and cannot say "healthy".

Educational project. Not for clinical use.
